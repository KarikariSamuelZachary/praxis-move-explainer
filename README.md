# Praxis AI-Powered Chess Learning App

Praxis is a chess improvement workspace built around understanding, practice, and useful repetition.

It helps players learn from their games, train important positions, prepare for opponents, and build habits that carry into real games. The goal is not only to find a stronger move. The goal is to understand the decision, recognize the pattern again, and make a better choice next time.

## Problem

Chess tools often give players more information without giving them a clearer path to improvement.

- Engine numbers can show that a move was inaccurate without making the reason memorable.
- A long analysis can hide the few decisions that mattered most.
- Players may solve puzzles or study openings without connecting that work to their own games.
- Mistakes are easy to notice once and just as easy to repeat later.

As a result, players can spend a great deal of time studying without building stronger decisions over the board.

## Solution

Praxis connects analysis with practice in one continuous learning flow:

1. Review a game, choose a position, study an opening, or start a training session.
2. Identify the decisions and patterns that are most useful to understand.
3. Explain the idea in clear language so the lesson is easier to remember.
4. Practice the same type of decision in a focused setting.
5. Return to important mistakes and positions until recognition becomes more natural.

This makes Praxis a place to turn chess experience into repeatable skill, rather than a place to collect analysis and move on.

## Core Learning Loop (MVP)

1. The player brings in a completed game or chooses a training position.
2. Praxis highlights the moments that can teach something important.
3. The player studies what they were trying to do, what changed on the board, and which chess idea explains the result.
4. The position becomes an opportunity for active practice instead of a forgotten note.
5. Praxis brings useful positions back through review, repetition, and related training.
6. Over time, the player becomes quicker at spotting the same patterns in new games.

The loop is designed to make improvement visible in the quality of decisions, not only in a single game score.

## Key Features (MVP Scope)

### Game Review

Review completed games and focus on the decisions that shaped the result. Clear explanations help connect a move with the tactical, positional, or practical idea behind it, so the lesson is easier to apply in another game.

### Puzzles

Practice tactical positions that develop calculation, pattern recognition, and confidence under pressure. Regular puzzle work helps players see opportunities and dangers earlier instead of discovering them only after the position has changed.

### Mistake Cards

Turn important mistakes into focused training positions. Each position gives the player a chance to revisit a recurring weakness and replace a familiar error with a stronger decision.

### Woodpecker Method Training

Revisit important mistakes at useful intervals. Spaced repetition helps lessons stay available when they are needed, allowing players to build recognition instead of relying on a single review session.

### Endgame Training

Practice the positions where technique matters most. Endgame work builds confidence in converting advantages, holding difficult positions, understanding king and pawn play, and making precise decisions when there are fewer pieces on the board.

### Repertoire and Openings

Build opening plans that are connected to positions you can actually play. Repeated practice helps make your first decisions more familiar, shows where your preparation needs attention, and gives you a clearer plan when the game leaves memorized moves.

### Sparring and Scenario Training

Play against different strengths and styles to practice the decisions you want to improve. Opponent preparation helps you understand recurring tendencies, while scenario training gives you focused practice with attacks, defenses, conversions, traps, and other practical moments.

## Explicitly Out of Scope (MVP)

- Replacing online chess platforms for live games
- Treating one accuracy score as a complete measure of improvement
- Social competition as the center of the learning experience
- Adding more features before the core practice loop is useful and consistent

Praxis is designed to complement the games and communities players already use by helping them learn more from each one.

## Success Criteria

The MVP is successful if players can say:

- "I understand why that move worked or failed."
- "I recognized the same pattern in a new position."
- "I know what to practice after reviewing my game."
- "I am making fewer repeated mistakes."
- "I feel more prepared when a familiar position appears."

Improvement is the outcome. The tools and metrics exist to support better decisions, not to replace them.

## Learning Direction (High-Level)

Praxis keeps the board and the player's decisions at the center of the experience. Analysis helps find useful moments, explanations make those moments understandable, and repetition turns understanding into recognition.

The product is being shaped around a simple idea: every game should give the player something clear to learn, and every useful lesson should have a way to be practiced again.

## Monetization (Future)

Future paid options may support deeper training paths, larger practice libraries, and more personalized improvement. The value should come from clearer lessons, better practice, and progress that players can feel in their games.
